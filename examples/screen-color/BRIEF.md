# Same numbers, different lights
- The line: "Same orange. Different numbers."
- Angle: start on the thing people blame ("my screen's colours are off"), take one pixel apart as a machine, then swap one part, the colour filters, and watch the same numbers come out a different orange (also considered: a product photo that looks wrong on the customer's phone, told from the seller's side; a tour of panel technologies, LCD vs OLED, cut as a list with no turn)
- Audience and format: people who scroll tech on X, curious but not engineers; posted with a note about the anatomy skill · 1920×1080 · 93 s
- Explains: "why does the same colour look different on two screens?" · believed now: a file stores a colour, and a screen that shows it differently is badly made · can say after: a file stores three numbers that say how far to open three windows; what colour comes out depends on that screen's filters, so the same numbers make different colours, and colour management sends each screen its own numbers · cut list: OLED (emits rather than filters), white point and colour temperature, gamma curves, brightness and viewing angle, calibration hardware, ICC profile internals, HDR, sub-pixel layouts other than RGB stripes, polarisation physics beyond "a twist" · sources in SOURCES.md
- The device: one pixel of an LCD built as a bench instrument with the anatomy kit (base plate, backlight, rear polariser, a crystal cell per sub-pixel whose twist is a turning dial, a filter cartridge, a front bezel). It is the whole film: whole, exploded, pushed in on the dials, its cartridge swapped. Because the story is "a screen is a machine that obeys numbers", the picture is that machine obeying them: the numbers turn the dials, the dials let light through, the light passes the filters and blends. The two monitors that open the film are the same machine seen from across the room, and they close it, read correctly.
- Look: stands on the anatomy skill's own figures (its dial indicator and desk computer: dark paper, hairline ink, four-tone solids, one lit part) and on technical-manual exploded views (Teenage Engineering manuals) · takes the monochrome hairline machine, the exploded stack, the readout set in mono beside the figure, calm springs · this story changes one thing: colour is the subject, so the only colour in the film is the light the machine makes (R, G, B and the orange it blends to) · palette paper #131313, ink #E6E6E6, muted #8A8A8A, light: red #FF4B3A, green #3BE07A, blue #4A7BFF, oranges #E67832 (230·120·50) and #F7700F (the same numbers through deeper filters); colour events: act 1 orange appears out of the dark, act 2 it splits into three lights, act 3 it shifts redder, act 4 the two oranges become one · light: the kit's top-left key, plus the machine's own backlight glowing up through the stack · type: Inter 700 for the hero line (≈ 120 px), JetBrains Mono 600 for readouts (64–110 px), Inter 500 labels (34–40 px); captions Inter 600 46 px on a dark band
- Motion: an instrument on a bench, never a show: one change at a time, springs at ratio 0.8–0.9, the dials on the kit's plunger spring (≈ ratio 0.63, 3.3 Hz) · signature moves: the explode (layers rise one by one on their words), the dials turning to the numbers, the cartridge pulled out and a deeper one dropped in
